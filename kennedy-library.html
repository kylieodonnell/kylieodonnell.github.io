Kennedy Library occupancy counter

Case study

Making library capacity easier to understand.

Problem

While Kennedy Library was under renovation, students had to rely on temporary study spaces and scattered occupancy information. Existing campus counters were hard to find, inconsistent across spaces, and did not explain which parts of the library were actually usable. The goal was to make occupancy information visible from the Kennedy Library website so students could decide where to study before spending time searching in person.

Approach

I designed the counter around two levels of decision-making: a quick overview of all five floors, then a detailed floor view with area density and study room availability. The concept was based around Occuspace, a privacy-conscious occupancy sensing system that can provide real-time and historical count estimates through an API.

Implementation

I built the prototype with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Because I did not have access to live Occuspace devices, I modeled the data using rotating mock datasets that updated every ten seconds. Each dataset included floor occupancy percentages, study room availability, and mapped floor areas that could be represented as a heat map.

Testing and iteration

I tested the prototype with students and faculty through a feedback form covering usability, visual design, functionality, and overall usefulness. Early feedback showed that the main idea was useful, but some users were confused by the heat map colors, the relationship between floor colors and study rooms, and how to navigate back from a floor detail view. I added clearer instructions, refined the color explanations, removed the original back button pattern, and adjusted the UI to make the interaction feel more standard.

Outcome

Users rated the final prototype an average of 4.11 out of 5 across purpose, findability, layout, visual appeal, and usability. The most common positive feedback was that the counter would help students decide where to go during crowded periods, especially because it combined a whole-library overview with more specific floor and room information.

Future work

The next step would be connecting the interface to real Occuspace data and the library's study room booking system. I also planned for a denser sensor layout across the five floors so the heat maps could show area-level crowding instead of only general floor percentages.
